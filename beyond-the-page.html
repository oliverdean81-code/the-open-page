Beyond the Page

Use English to explore ideas, question assumptions and understand how people may see the same issue in different ways.

Learning to see more than one side

Beyond the Page connects English learning with the world around us. Explore important questions, compare international experiences, examine arguments and develop your own informed response.

The Big Question Perspectives Around the World The Argument Your Voice
01 · THE BIG QUESTION

Start with a question worth thinking about

Each feature begins with an open question connected to literature, news, science, history, society or everyday life. There may be several reasonable answers, and learners are encouraged to explain their thinking.

FEATURED QUESTION
Who should be responsible for protecting the environment?

Individuals, businesses, governments — or all of us?

A question is a starting point, not a test with one predetermined answer.

02 · PERSPECTIVES AROUND THE WORLD

Explore the issue through different experiences

People's views can be shaped by where they live, their work and family experiences, their history, their access to resources and the choices available to them. People in the same country may disagree, while people in very different places may share similar concerns.

We explore contexts and lived experiences without suggesting that everyone in a place or group thinks alike. Each perspective should be supported by reliable sources, examples or first-hand contributions.

Island and coastal communities

How do sea-level rise, storms, coastal livelihoods and the cost of adaptation affect local priorities?

People in large cities

How do public transport, housing, air quality and access to green spaces shape environmental choices?

Rural and farming communities

How can communities balance food production, employment, water quality and protection of ecosystems?

Businesses and workers

Who pays for cleaner technology and changes to production, and how might those changes affect jobs and prices?

Governments and public services

How should public policy balance long-term environmental goals with immediate social and economic needs?

Young people and future generations

How should decisions made today take account of people who will live with their consequences tomorrow?

These are lenses for investigation, not generalisations about entire countries or populations.

03 · THE ARGUMENT

Examine claims, evidence and alternatives

Good discussion involves more than stating an opinion. Learners practise identifying claims, asking what evidence supports them, recognising assumptions and considering counterarguments.

Make a claim

What exactly is being argued? Is the statement specific enough to examine?

Look for evidence

What reliable information supports the claim? Who produced it, and what might be missing?

Identify assumptions

What is the argument taking for granted? Would those assumptions apply in every context?

Consider a counterargument

What might a thoughtful person with a different view say? How could you respond fairly?

Useful academic expressions

  • From this perspective…
  • By contrast…
  • This argument appears to overlook…
  • The available evidence suggests…
  • Nevertheless, an alternative interpretation is…
04 · YOUR VOICE

Develop and share your own response

After exploring the issue, learners form their own view. The aim is not to agree with a particular position, but to express an idea clearly, support it with reasons and engage respectfully with other perspectives.

Your first challenge

Should responsibility for protecting the environment fall mainly on individuals, businesses or governments? Explain your view and include one reason someone might disagree with you.

Suggested activity: B2–C1 · Write 120–180 words.

How to prepare a strong response

  1. State your position clearly.
  2. Give reasons and relevant evidence or examples.
  3. Acknowledge another perspective.
  4. Explain why you reach your conclusion.

Student contributions may be invited for future features and reviewed by an editor before publication. A public submission form and moderation process can be added when we are ready to receive contributions.

How a Beyond the Page feature works

  1. Read or watch a short introduction to the topic.
  2. Explore different experiences and viewpoints around the world.
  3. Study language for comparing, evaluating and disagreeing respectfully.
  4. Examine arguments and evidence.
  5. Write or record your own response.

Features can be adapted for B1, B2 and C1 learners, with more demanding texts and analysis at higher levels.
