Beyond the Page

Use English to explore ideas, question assumptions and understand how people may see the same issue in different ways.

Learning to see more than one side

Beyond the Page goes beyond vocabulary and comprehension. It invites learners to connect language with the world around them: to consider evidence, listen to different experiences, ask thoughtful questions and form their own reasoned views.

FEATURED SERIES

Perspectives Around the World

People's views are shaped by many things: where they live, their work and family experiences, their history, their access to resources and the choices available to them. Two people in the same country may disagree, while people in very different places may share similar concerns.

This series starts with one shared question and explores how it might look from different contexts. Learners compare experiences and evidence rather than assuming that everyone in a country thinks alike.

OUR STARTING QUESTION
Who should be responsible for protecting the environment?

Individuals, businesses, governments — or all of us?

Look at the question through different lenses

For example, an environmental issue may raise different practical questions for people in different circumstances. These are starting points for investigation, not claims that everyone in a particular place holds the same opinion.

Island and coastal communities

How do sea-level rise, storms, coastal livelihoods and the cost of adaptation affect local priorities?

People in large cities

How do public transport, housing, air quality and access to green spaces shape environmental choices?

Rural and farming communities

How can communities balance food production, employment, water quality and protection of ecosystems?

Businesses and workers

Who pays for cleaner technology and changes to production, and how might those changes affect jobs and prices?

Governments and public services

How should public policy balance long-term environmental goals with immediate social and economic needs?

Young people and future generations

How should decisions made today take account of people who will live with their consequences tomorrow?

How learners take part

  1. Read a short, accessible introduction to the issue.
  2. Explore several perspectives, supported by reliable sources and real examples.
  3. Learn useful language for comparing, agreeing, disagreeing and qualifying an argument.
  4. Answer comprehension and critical-thinking questions.
  5. Write a short response explaining their own view and acknowledging another perspective.

Future editions can include articles, interviews, maps, short videos and contributions from people with direct experience. Student contributions can be moderated before publication.

Try the first discussion

Should responsibility for protecting the environment fall mainly on individuals, businesses or governments? Explain your view. Include one reason someone might disagree with you.

Suggested activity: B2–C1 · Write 120–180 words.
